Academic Project Overview

About the EchoCheck Project

A college mini project investigating natural language processing methodologies for detecting fabricated content in digital journalism.

Problem Statement & Motivation

The rapid proliferation of digital misinformation, algorithmic clickbait, and fabricated political narratives poses a severe challenge to democratic discourse, public health policies, and collective societal trust. With social networks amplifying sensationalism at scale, manual fact-checking organizations struggle to keep pace with the volume of generated content.

EchoCheck was conceived as an academic engineering endeavor to explore how mathematical feature engineering (TF-IDF unigrams and bigrams) combined with calibrated support vector machines and logistic regression can serve as a rapid, lightweight first-line defense against automated and synthetic disinformation.

Dataset: Kaggle Fake and Real News Corpus

EchoCheck is trained on the benchmark Kaggle Fake and Real News dataset (ISOT Research Lab), comprising thousands of authenticated articles from credible wire reports alongside thousands of verified fabricated stories originating from debunked fake news websites.

  • 24,000+ Articles balanced across authentic journalism and verified fabricated reports.
  • Dateline Stripping Preprocessing: Prevents model bias towards publisher signatures.
  • 15,000 N-Gram Dimensions: Capturing syntactic word associations and rhetorical markers.

Academic Limitations & Ethical Considerations

1. Stylistic vs. Factual Grounding

TF-IDF models analyze rhetorical and lexical style rather than querying an external live knowledge graph. Sophisticated disinformation that mimics formal journalistic cadence can sometimes bypass lexical classifiers, which is why multi-source human oversight remains essential.

2. Temporal Concept Drift

Political figures, geopolitical entities, and terminology evolve over time. Academic models trained on historical corpora require periodic retraining to maintain efficacy on contemporary events and emerging phraseology.
